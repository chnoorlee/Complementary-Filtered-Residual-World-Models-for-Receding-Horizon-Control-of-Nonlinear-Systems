# Research Definition v1

## Topic and scope

This project studies a **state-space world model**: an internal, action-conditioned predictive model of controlled physical dynamics. It does not train or evaluate a control policy, and it does not claim to reproduce complete DreamerV3, IRIS, or TD-MPC2 agents. The empirical object is the transition model under a shared supervised rollout protocol.

The central setting is deliberately difficult but reproducible: observations may be noisy or missing, and the physical parameters at deployment may differ from the training distribution. A differentiable nominal simulator is available but misspecified. This represents a practical middle ground between purely data-driven dynamics and an exact simulator.

## Primary research question

Can a world model that **predicts the local reliability of both a nominal-physics expert and a learned dynamics expert, then gates their contributions during autoregressive rollout**, improve long-horizon accuracy and uncertainty calibration under observation corruption and parameter shift relative to recent world-model transition backbones and a fixed physics-guided neural model?

## Two core ideas only

1. **Reliability-gated physics residual fusion.** The model predicts branch-specific errors and uses them to combine a differentiable nominal step with a learned residual/data expert. The gate is trained against observed branch errors on training trajectories rather than treated as an uninterpretable attention weight.
2. **Rollout-aware calibrated training.** Multi-horizon Gaussian likelihood and reliability supervision are optimized jointly, so the reported uncertainty is tied to forecast error during free rollout rather than only teacher-forced one-step error.

## Prioritized subquestions

1. **Primary — long-horizon accuracy:** Does RGP-WM reduce normalized RMSE at horizon 25 across all 15 prespecified system-regime cells?
2. **Reliability:** Does the predicted 90% interval achieve lower calibration error and competitive sharpness under corruption and parameter shift?
3. **Physical plausibility:** Does reliability gating reduce normalized one-step dynamics defect and catastrophic rollout divergence without hard-projecting predictions to a possibly wrong simulator?
4. **Mechanism:** Do reliability supervision, nominal physics, and multi-step training each contribute in the expected metric family?
5. **Cost:** Are parameter count, wall-clock training time, and inference latency compatible with the matched recent backbones?

## Statistical unit and estimand

- Data unit: one simulator trajectory generated from an independently sampled initial condition, control sequence, and physical-parameter draw.
- Training replication unit: one paired random seed, with identical trajectory splits for all methods.
- Primary estimand: paired difference in mean normalized horizon-25 RMSE between RGP-WM and the best comparator, averaged over the 15 prespecified cells.
- Uncertainty: 95% hierarchical bootstrap confidence interval, resampling seeds and trajectories; cell-level tests use Holm correction.

## Systems and deployment regimes

Five controlled systems are planned: damped harmonic oscillator, forced Duffing oscillator, controlled Van der Pol oscillator, torque-driven pendulum, and cart-pole. Each is generated by an RK4 reference solver. The model receives a deliberately misspecified, cheaper nominal integrator.

Three evaluation regimes per system yield 15 cells:

1. in-distribution clean observations;
2. observation corruption (Gaussian noise plus missing values);
3. physical-parameter shift combined with observation corruption.

## Falsifiable success and failure

- H1 success: >=10% improvement in the prespecified aggregate normalized horizon-25 RMSE over the best comparator, with the paired 95% confidence interval excluding zero improvement.
- H2 success: lower aggregate Gaussian NLL and interval calibration error than the best comparator, without more than a 5% degradation in one-step RMSE.
- H3 success: lower catastrophic rollout rate and normalized dynamics defect than the best data-only comparator.
- Mechanistic success: removing reliability supervision, the physics expert, or multi-step loss worsens its prespecified metric in at least four of five systems.
- Pivot: if H1 fails, the manuscript cannot claim superior robust prediction; it must either narrow to calibration/mechanistic findings or redesign the method and rerun all affected cells.

## Hardware and feasibility boundary

Verified hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Laptop GPU (8,151 MiB reported by `nvidia-smi`), AMD Ryzen 9 9955HX (16 cores/32 threads), approximately 16 GB system RAM, Python and MiKTeX. This supports compact state-space models and hundreds of short runs, but not a credible image-scale reproduction of full Atari/robotics agents. The experiment therefore evaluates transition backbones under a transparent protocol.
